Privacy Policy

Bite is a news reader. It shows you short summaries of news stories and sends you to the publisher to read them. This page explains exactly what it stores about you, why, and how to make it stop.

Last updated 20 August 2026 · Applies to the Bite iOS app.

The short version.

Bite has no advertising, no analytics SDK, and no trackers. It does not sell or share your data with anyone, and it never links what you do in Bite to what you do anywhere else. You can use it without giving an email address. Everything it stores about you exists to rank your feed, and you can delete all of it from inside the app in two taps.

What Bite stores, and why

DataWhenWhy
An anonymous account ID On first launch, always — including if you continue as a guest. It is what your saves and swipes belong to, and what the database uses to make sure you can only ever read your own. It is a random identifier; it is not derived from your device, your phone number, or you.
Your email address Only if you choose to create an account. Guests never provide one. To sign you in with a one-time code, and to let your saves follow you to a new phone. There is no password. It is never used for marketing — Bite sends no email other than your sign-in codes.
Your topics and region When you pick them in onboarding or Profile. To shape your feed. Region is a preference you choose from a list, not your location. Bite requests no location permission and cannot read your position.
Your swipes, saves and opens Every time you swipe or open a card. This is the recommendation engine. Bite averages the stories you read and save into a taste model and ranks your feed against it. This is the one genuinely revealing thing Bite holds — a record of what news you chose to read — and it is stored under your account ID and readable by nobody else.
Stories you follow When you tap the bell on a story. To collect later coverage of that story into its timeline.
Impressions and click-throughs When a card is shown to you, and when you tap through to a publisher. So Bite can tell publishers how many readers it sent them. Reported to them only as totals — a publisher is told "1,240 impressions, 96 click-throughs, 41 readers", never who.
Your appearance choice If you change it in Profile. Stored on your device only. It never leaves your phone.

What Bite does not collect

  • No location. No advertising identifier (IDFA). No contacts, photos, calendar, microphone or camera.
  • No device fingerprint, and no third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDK — there is no Firebase, no Google Analytics, no Meta SDK, no attribution SDK in the app.
  • No cross-app or cross-site tracking of any kind. Under Apple's definition, Bite does not track, and it does not ask for permission to, because it has nothing to ask for.

Where it is stored

Bite's backend runs on Supabase (Postgres), hosted in Sydney, Australia (ap-southeast-2). If you are in India, the EU/EEA, the UK, or anywhere else, your data is transferred to and stored in Australia. Transfers are protected by Supabase's data processing terms and standard contractual clauses; Australia is not the subject of an EU adequacy decision, and that is disclosed here rather than left to be discovered.

Everything is transmitted over TLS, and every table enforces row-level security so one reader's rows cannot be read by another.

Who else sees anything

  • Supabase — hosting, database and authentication. A processor acting on instructions; it does not use your data for itself.
  • OpenRouter — writes the short summaries. It is sent news article text only. No account ID, no email, and nothing about any reader is ever sent to it. Summaries are generated once for everybody, not per reader.
  • Publishers — when you tap a story you go to their website, inside an in-app browser. From that moment you are on their site and their privacy policy applies, including any cookies or trackers they use. Bite does not tell them who you are, but it cannot control what their page does once it loads.
  • Apple — distributes the app and handles purchases (there are none). Apple's own privacy policy applies to the App Store.

Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared for advertising. Bite carries no advertising and has no revenue.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and everything under it — until you delete it. There is no automatic expiry, because your reading history is what makes your feed good.
  • Article text fetched for summarising — deleted as soon as the summary is written, and in any case within 48 hours.
  • Impression and click-through records — kept for publisher reporting. When you delete your account these are anonymised, not deleted: the link to your account is removed, leaving a count with nothing identifying attached. This is the one exception to deletion and it is stated here deliberately.

Deleting your data

In the app: Profile → Delete account.

This permanently removes your account, your saved stories, your entire reading history and the taste model built from it, your topics and region, and every story you follow. It happens immediately and cannot be undone. No email, no form, no waiting.

Signing out is different and does not delete anything — it leaves your data with the account so you can sign back in.

Your rights

Bite grants the following to every reader, wherever you live. Rather than giving EU readers one set of rights and everyone else another, the stronger set is applied to everybody:

  • Access — a copy of everything held about you.
  • Correction — fix anything inaccurate.
  • Erasure — delete it all (in-app, as above).
  • Portability — your data in a machine-readable file.
  • Restriction and objection — including objecting to the recommendation ranking; ask and your taste model will be cleared.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, without affecting what was done before you withdrew it.
  • Nominate someone to exercise these rights on your behalf in the event of death or incapacity (DPDP Act s.14).

Email bitenewsapp@gmail.com and you will get an answer within 30 days. If you are unhappy with the response you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India, or to your local supervisory authority if you are in the EU/EEA or the UK.

Legal bases (EU/EEA and UK readers)

  • Contract — your account, saves, and trackers: without these the app cannot do what you asked it to.
  • Legitimate interests — the recommendation ranking and aggregate publisher reporting, balanced against the fact that the data stays inside Bite, is never sold, is never combined with outside sources, and can be erased on demand.
  • Consent — where you have given it, withdrawable at any time.

Children

Bite is rated 17+ and is not intended for children. It shows unfiltered news and opens publisher websites in a browser. Bite does not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18, and does not carry out behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children — it carries no advertising at all. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and it will be deleted.
